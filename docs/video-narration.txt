A shop and a quiet shopper
Imagine running a furniture shop with products ready to sell, while a shopper who could buy from you has gone quiet.

A reason to reach out
In our simulated market, this shopper hasn't bought for sixteen days. Sending everyone a discount is easy, but finding a recommendation worth sending takes evidence.

Connect the evidence
So Market Metro first connects the shopper, product and payment records. Its ontology keeps split merchant orders tied to one checkout, and holds any links it can't trust.

Build the campaign
With that evidence, the agent examines demand and stock, chooses twenty eight days of training data, and asks local tools to fit two models for a reactivation campaign.

Test before sending
The first idea doesn't get a free pass. A verifier tests both rankings in separate development markets and selects the one that earns more while protecting merchants.

The recommendation becomes a purchase
On day thirty five, the selected recommendation reaches our shopper. They buy a ninety six dollar side table, and it arrives the next day; fixed rules left that same request unfilled.

A payment you can trace
We mirror that order once through PayPal Sandbox. The completed capture records the fee and merchant proceeds, then returns a receipt that links all the way back to the purchase.

What changed
Across this twelve store simulation, the campaign delivers sixteen extra retained purchases, over a thousand dollars in merchant contribution, and three point four seven percent more modeled PayPal net.

Follow the full trail
Judges can follow the whole trail in AG Grid, from model training and the agent's decision to the PayPal receipt, instead of taking the result on trust.

Agentic commerce that learns
Market Metro turns observed activity into tested campaigns and traceable payments: agentic commerce that learns.
