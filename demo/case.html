    THE RULES OF THIS EXPERIMENT

    Every material assumption is visible.

    1. Payments already happened. Two offices have four previously paid supply-kit orders of $250 each. The payments are captured and settled. This cohort cannot be cancelled by voiding authorizations.
    2. Only $300 is recoverable. The merchant already spent $700, which is assumed unrecoverable. $100 available cash and $200 reserve remain. No other assets or later debt recovery are assumed.
    3. PayPal covers the gap. For this eligible-refund scenario, assume PayPal makes customers whole and bears any amount that cannot be recovered from the merchant. This is a scenario assumption, not a blanket description of PayPal policy.
    4. The replacement costs $160. Four units, $40 each, one upfront batch. Releasing $60 lets Mara pay it and leaves $140 backing any later refund. No other supplier, credit, or financing is available within the deadline.
    5. Success is uncertain. The adjustable default is 85%. Failure means all four orders require full refunds and the replacement payment cannot be recovered. Success means all four are delivered with no additional returns during the modeled horizon.
    6. Future benefit is separate. Conditional on success, assume $500 of new volume over 30 days and a 2% net contribution rate. These are authored inputs, not PayPal prices or booked revenue.
    7. Authority stays bounded. The authored demo cap is a $60 release and $900 maximum uncovered refund loss in the failure branch. Expected uncovered refunds must also decrease; speculative future contribution cannot override that rule. A simulated reviewer decides. Fresh stock and evidence are checked again before execution.
    8. The operation is local. Agents, reserve controls, refunds, and receipts are scripted simulations. No public PayPal reserve-override API is connected. Native Sandbox payment integration comes later.
    9. Basic supply and demand. Each office starts with two kits and uses one every eight simulation seconds after play starts, stopping when a branch executes. Request slips replay existing paid orders. The van moves replacement stock through pickup, packing and office arrivals. Travel time is compressed for the demo; it does not forecast real delivery timing. No extra payments are created.

    WHY PAYPAL WOULD USE THIS

    Protect customers. Investigate the recovery.

    The intended user is a PayPal merchant-reserve reviewer. Aftershock investigates whether changing a reserve could reduce the amount PayPal ultimately has to cover when a merchant cannot fulfill already-paid orders.

    Unverified supplier→Agent evidence→Reviewed reserve choice→Refund exposure

    The supply operation makes the causal mechanism visible: money releases buying capacity; stock and supplier performance determine whether that capacity becomes fulfilled orders. The comparison includes the extra loss if the attempt fails.

    The user controls the economic assumptions. Evidence can show that the route exists; it cannot establish the invented success probability or prove real business savings. The current scripted investigation illustrates the intended workflow and does not demonstrate an LLM advantage.
