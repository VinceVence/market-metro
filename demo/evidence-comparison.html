HONEST IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

Today: a scripted workflow. The agent claim still needs proving.

This is neither a trained regression model nor an autonomous AI team. Fixed code chooses carrier lookup → office lookup, validates IDs and applies a reserve formula. A conventional workflow can perform those same steps and match the result. Agentic behavior and ML are different dimensions: a predictive model could be one tool inside an investigator.

Where ML would fit

Predict delivery delay, dispute risk or likely loss from known features. A score can prioritize cases. It does not by itself decide which missing document to request next.

Where an agent could fit

Choose the next useful source, interpret messy carrier and merchant messages, revise an explanation after contradictory evidence, and stop or escalate when further checks are not worth their cost. Those capabilities are proposed, not running here.

What stays deterministic

Order matching, monetary arithmetic, reserve limits and approval permissions. The agent may propose an investigation and recommendation; it cannot invent proof or bypass the reviewer.

A concrete future agent decision: a carrier says “delivered,” the office says “only part arrived,” and the merchant supplies a batch manifest. The investigator chooses to reconcile that manifest before paying for more carrier lookups, splits confirmed and unresolved units, then requests independent proof only for the unresolved part. It revises its plan as evidence changes. Start with one investigator plus deterministic checks; extra agent roles need their own measured benefit.

The test we still need: compare an adaptive investigator against a fixed retrieval workflow and an ML-prioritized workflow on unfamiliar cases, under equal tool budgets. Measure false releases, unnecessary holds, recovered evidence and total cost. Four role labels or an LLM-written explanation alone would not establish an advantage.

Try Same scripted review in the reference settings. It uses identical retrieval, policy and cost on both sides; the expected benefit is exactly zero.

Costs, controls and simulation assumptions

Hypothetical rates, not PayPal’s pricing or actual model cost. Changing settings resets both worlds. No LLM is called.

Identical inputs, policy-dependent consequences

Both worlds share all orders and gross amounts, the saved receipt reference, $50 starting cash per merchant, supplier opening stock, procurement prices, and outcome/evidence draws keyed by seed, merchant or kit, shift and channel. Separate reserves and cash change procurement and later deliveries. Resulting evidence may differ because deliveries differ; the random availability rules stay matched. No outcome is revealed to investigators before its record is requested.

Fees and contribution

Each generated order pays an authored 3% fee; the single saved receipt uses its recorded fee. Fees are retained after refunds in this model. Processing cost is charged on each gross payment; review cost is charged once per merchant per approved investigated shift, even when no funds are released. A static full hold or initial-feed calculation has no incremental investigation cost. The same-review control charges the same cost on both sides.

Net contribution = retained fees − processing cost − provider-funded refund shortfalls − review cost. The relative benefit is reviewed contribution minus reference contribution. Freed merchant reserves are not revenue. Merchant-funded refunds are not provider losses. Costs are applied to the provider accounting only; merchant funds are never charged twice.

Limits of the result

This fixed-demand, three-shift experiment has no churn, retention uplift, later chargebacks, expiring reserves or terminal value. It does not calculate PayPal’s full accounting profit. The 100% net review hold, 20% verified-gross buffer, synthetic source records and provider shortfall coverage are authored assumptions. The cached sandbox receipt proves one earlier test payment, not delivery or production benefit.
