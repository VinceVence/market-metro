Fees, costs, churn and comparison rules

All rates below are illustrative inputs. They are not a published PayPal fee schedule or calibrated churn model. Changes apply only when you reset both worlds.

How the comparison stays matched

Both worlds start with the same individual merchant wallets, 20% reserves, operating costs, potential office demand, stock quantities, procurement prices and supplier success assumptions. The three shocks are copied to both sides. Delivery and churn draws are indexed by seed, merchant ID, shift and event type, so a skipped payment cannot shift the other world’s random sequence. Stock pools and ledgers are separate.

Fees and costs

Percentage fees are collected on captured gross payments and deducted from merchant cash before procurement. The reserve percentage is applied to gross payments. Returned fees, when enabled, are credited to the merchant before refund coverage is calculated. Processing cost is an assumed PayPal expense on captured gross volume and is not returned after refunds. Agent review cost is charged once per active merchant per agent-policy shift, including paused merchants who still need review. The fixed rule has no incremental agent review cost.

Merchant churn

At 1× sensitivity, the departure probability is 2% + 20% × unfulfilled paid share + 15% if payments were paused this shift or are announced paused next shift + 10% × reserve pressure. Reserve pressure is zero at 20% held and reaches one at 40%. Multiply the total by the sensitivity setting, capped at 100%. There is no service-failure term when no orders were charged.

Departure is checked after current deliveries and refunds settle. The merchant withdraws its remaining funds, leaves permanently within this replay, and generates no subsequent PayPal captures or fees. We track lost potential volume from churn separately from active merchants whose payments are paused.

What the net number includes

Net contribution = fees collected − fees returned − processing cost − assumed eligible refund coverage − agent review cost. Agent benefit is agent contribution minus fixed contribution. Churn affects later fees and claims through changed volume; there is no extra lifetime-value penalty or bonus. Departures in the final shift change retention, while any fees beyond the three-shift horizon are excluded.

The original scripted coordinator remains a service-and-refund-risk heuristic. This comparison evaluates it; it has not been retuned to guarantee positive net benefit. Both strategies may lose money. Potential demand is matched, but realized captures differ because of payment restrictions and churn. This is one synthetic paired seed, not production ROI or proof of an LLM advantage.
